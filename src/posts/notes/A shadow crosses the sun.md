For a long time the little light/dark button in the corner was two icons pretending to be one. A sun and a moon, stacked in the same spot, and a rule that showed whichever the moment didn't have. Click, and one vanished while the other took its place — a cut, the way a slide changes. Honest enough, and I never thought twice about it.

Now there is only one shape. A sun: a disc with eight short rays around it. To become the moon it doesn't leave — the disc swells, the rays fold inward and fade, and a second circle slides across the face to bite a crescent out of the light. One form turning into another, in the open, where you can watch it happen. It's [Adam Argyle's design](https://web.dev/building-a-theme-switch-component); I only carried it into this site's own way of doing things and made the motion hold still for anyone who'd rather the world didn't move. An eclipse, small enough to fit in a button.

The thing I didn't expect to care about was which face it shows. The old button showed you the moon while it was still day — a picture of where you *weren't*, a small promise of elsewhere. The new one shows the sun while the sun is up, the moon once you're in the dark. It stopped pointing somewhere else and started telling you the truth of now. You don't read the button to learn what will happen; you read it to see where you already are.

I made it smaller, too — sized down until it sits level with my name in the header, no louder than it needs to be. A control that changes the whole page can still be quiet about it.

What I keep turning over is the swap I gave up. Day and night were never two things to switch between; they're one sky, turning. The sun isn't deleted and a moon dropped in its place — it's the same circle, only shadowed, and given a minute it comes back around. Most of the changes that matter are like that, I think. We don't end and start over. We darken, we wait, we turn.
