Today I went back for a room that closed eleven years ago.

This Is My Jam let you post one song at a time — only one, held up like a small flag until you chose the next — and a handful of people would drift past, leave a heart, now and then a word. It shut its doors in 2015. I'd already rebuilt the songs themselves from a page I happened to have saved, but I assumed the talk around them was gone for good. A jam is a lonely thing without the people who heard it.

The Internet Archive had kept the room. Not the front of it — the service redirects everything now to anonymous song pages — but tucked in the markup, under a class I'd been overlooking, my own captions were still there. Younger, blunter than I'd remember writing them. Under Harvey Milk's "Crush Them All", the day after the 2014 election: *"More blues from the abyss. The fascists got 13% in Sweden yesterday. This will not stand. ¡No Pasarán!"* The abyss got deeper since. The sentence reads like a letter from someone who didn't know that yet.

And the company is back too — the likes, the comments, faces I half-remember pulled out of a dead photo server one at a time. mwackett, all caps, *EXCITED EXCITED EXCITED* about a Converge single, then back the next day to say he'd bought tickets. A small, specific enthusiasm, preserved by accident. The website threw all of this away; some patient crawler kept it. Now it sits under each jam again, where it belongs — proof that for a while the songs were heard, and that being heard was the whole point.
