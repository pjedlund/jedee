I wanted something simple: finish a run, and have it show up on my own site, no phone unlocked, no button pressed. It turns out this is precisely the one thing Apple won't allow. Health data is sealed the moment the screen locks, and nothing — no app, no shortcut, no clever workaround — gets to read a finished workout until you unlock the phone again. The watch remembers everything. It just won't tell anyone unless you're standing there to ask.

So today wasn't really about the watch. It was about turning around and looking at five years of races that had only ever lived on Strava — the times, the splits, one photo of each course posted and then left to scroll away. A hundred and nine of them, once I'd sorted the real starts from the ordinary evening runs: park-O evenings in Trelleborg and Malmö, three-day events in Halland and Gotland, night orienteering through Bokskogen with a headlamp, one run to a start line logged as its own separate activity, as if even my watch knew the difference between arriving and beginning.

None of them have their maps yet. Those are still sitting in years of photos I haven't gone back for. That's next.
