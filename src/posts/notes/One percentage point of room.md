The job was supposed to take twenty minutes. The mega-menu — the panel that drops down and lists every kind of thing on this site, with a count beside each one — had icons I didn't like. Too dim in dark mode, too loud in light. I wanted them brighter in one and quieter in the other, which is the sort of adjustment you make by nudging a number and looking, then nudging it back.

Then I asked a second question, almost in passing: is the text in there actually readable enough?

The counts were not. Not close, either — 2.76 to 1 against the panel in light mode, where the standard asks for 4.5. Dark was 3.27. These are the numbers that decide whether someone with ordinary middle-aged eyes, or a cheap screen, or sunlight coming in from the side, can read *120* next to *Jams* without leaning in. Every one of those numbers is the only place its information exists. There's no label repeating it, nothing else on the row that says how many.

The fix turned out not to be a number at all. It was a decision I couldn't delegate to the arithmetic. The colours in that panel are all mixtures — some percentage of the text colour blended toward the page — and the first mixture that passes in light mode is eighty percent. Which is exactly what the *label* beside the count was already using. So the only way to make the number legible was to give it the same colour as the word next to it, and flatten the small difference in weight that made the row read as a name with a quantity attached, rather than two things of equal importance sitting in a row.

We moved both instead. The label went to full strength, the count took the label's old value, and the two-step arrangement survived one notch darker than before.

That's when I asked the question I actually want to remember. Could it be softer than that? Could I claw back a little of the quietness I'd just spent?

So we swept the whole range, every percentage from fifty to a hundred, and measured what each one landed at. Light mode runs out at seventy-nine percent. I was sitting at eighty.

One point. That was the entire remaining budget.

I had been thinking about contrast the way I think about most rules — a test at the end, a thing you pass or fail, a gate. It isn't. It's a budget, and it gets spent long before you arrive at the thing you're adjusting. I spent mine years ago, when I chose a page that isn't quite white and a text colour that isn't quite black, because I liked how they sat together and because I still do. Every soft, quiet, tasteful decision after that one draws down the same account. By the time I got to the numbers in a dropdown menu, there was a single percentage point left in it, and I had no idea.

Dark mode had twelve points spare, so the counts there could relax a little, and did. That isn't dark mode being more considerate. It's just that my dark palette happens to put more distance between the text and the background than my light one does. Virtue had nothing to do with it. It was already true before I looked.

I checked the obvious escape routes on the way past. Making the panel whiter buys two points, which is nothing. Making the text bigger buys a great deal, because past a certain size the standard relaxes what it asks for — but that's a different panel than the one I've got, and I wasn't in the mood to redesign it to win an argument with a colour.

The menu looks very close to how it looked this morning. A shade darker in two places, a bit of blue in the icons that wasn't there before. Nobody will notice, which is the correct outcome. What changed isn't the panel. It's that I now know where its walls are, and that I was standing against one of them the whole time, in the dark, wondering why there wasn't more room.
